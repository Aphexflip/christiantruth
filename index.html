The debate rules.

A site built only to say “religion bad” is easy to ignore. A site that consistently applies the same standard to believers and skeptics can become a reference people actually trust.

1. Attack the propositionReligion is a protected identity for real people. The target here is claims, doctrines, texts, institutions, and evidence—not harassment of believers.
2. Steelman before rebuttalUse the strongest defensible version of the religious argument. Beating a weak caricature proves nothing.
3. Separate four questionsWhat does the text say? What probably happened historically? What follows philosophically? Is it morally defensible? Do not blur them.
4. “Unknown” is allowedNot knowing a natural explanation does not prove God. But lack of proof against God does not prove atheism either.
5. Link primary evidenceWhere possible, show scripture, papers, museum evidence, university scholarship, or reference works—not screenshots of someone else's opinion.
6. Correct ourselves publiclyA visible correction policy is an advantage. Religious truth claims should be falsifiable; our claims should be too.
